A morning spoonful of olive oil: tradition, evidence and measure

Taking olive oil in the morning may be a valued family ritual, but the ritual itself should be distinguished from a scientifically demonstrated health effect.

Olivamore Editoryal24 August 20266 min read
In brief

Research usually examines olive oil within a whole dietary pattern or as a replacement for other fats. No unique advantage of taking it in the morning or on an empty stomach over using the same amount in food has been established.

A tradition is not a treatment

A practice passed down through generations may have cultural value, but that does not establish that it prevents or treats a condition. A morning spoonful can be described as a personal preference for adults who enjoy it, not as a requirement or cure.

What does the research actually study?

Much of the stronger research examines olive oil within a wider dietary pattern or as a replacement for other fats. PREDIMED, for example, did not test one spoonful taken neat on an empty stomach; it assessed a whole Mediterranean-diet intervention involving education, multiple food choices and olive oil.

No unique morning advantage has been established

Current evidence does not establish that drinking olive oil in the morning has a unique advantage over using the same amount in food. The overall diet, the amount used and what the oil replaces matter more than the clock. Claims such as “works better on an empty stomach” or a precise multiplier for absorption should be avoided.

The spoonful still counts

Olive oil is a source of unsaturated fat, but like all fats it is energy-dense. If a morning spoonful is added on top of all the oil used later, total energy intake also rises. A more balanced approach is to count it as a replacement for another fat.

You do not have to drink it neat

The same measured amount can be used on tomatoes, eggs, toast, vegetables or pulses. This keeps olive oil within a meal, where it contributes flavour and culinary function rather than being treated like a supplement.

Practical checklist

  • ●If you enjoy the ritual, count it within your daily use.
  • ●Use it in place of another fat rather than adding an unlimited extra.
  • ●Do not treat empty-stomach use as a requirement.
  • ●Ask a health professional about reflux, gallbladder, medicines or special dietary needs.
  • ●Avoid detox, fat-burning and disease-treatment promises.
Important note

This content is not medical advice. Claims that olive oil “detoxifies”, “cleanses the liver”, causes weight loss or treats constipation or another condition are not supported here. Commercial health claims may be used only in the exact authorised form and conditions for the destination market.
